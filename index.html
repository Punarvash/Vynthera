The problem

One prescription, five questions, a dozen websites.

The answers exist in public data. They are scattered across government sites, journals and plan documents that nobody has time to search during a visit.

Does it work for this patient’s condition?

Hundreds of studies in PubMed, of very different quality and funding.

Is this use FDA-approved?

Buried in the indications of the FDA label, sometimes under another name.

What will my patient pay?

Spread across NADAC, Medicare spending and thousands of plan formularies.

Who should I refer to?

Experience hides in CMS prescriber data; contacts in the NPI Registry.

How do I explain it simply?

Medication Guides and MedlinePlus pages, written for different readers.

Questions

Frequently asked.

Where does the information come from?

From public US sources: the FDA (labels and Drugs@FDA), the National Library of Medicine (PubMed, RxNorm, MedlinePlus), ClinicalTrials.gov, CMS Medicare datasets and plan formularies, Medicaid NADAC prices, the VA price list and the NPI Registry.

Does Vynthera use AI?

AI is optional and limited to summarizing and plain-language writing, and it is labelled where used. Approval checks, certainty ratings, funding and costs come from the source data and fixed rules.

Is it medical advice?

No. Vynthera is an information tool for licensed clinicians and does not replace clinical judgement. Every claim links to its source so it can be verified.

How current is the data?

Prices, Medicare plan coverage and prescriber data are imported every week. Labels, studies and trials are looked up live and cached for a short time.

Does it store patient information?

No. There is no patient database. Patient details entered for a letter stay in the browser and are not sent to Vynthera’s servers or to any AI service.
